This one's going to be a looooooooong one.  
This is my all time favorite _soloyolo_<Fn n={1} /> and I hope you enjoy reading it as much as I enjoyed living it. There's also a ton of tech behind the scenes!

I had been dreaming about a trip like this for a good few years - a Gujarat-Rajasthan trip, in my own vehicle, with nowhere to be, and free of any worry in the world.  
Everything aligned in Dec 2023 and a happier chubbier me got to experience it all.

Lasted 17 days, 5500 km, starting from Bangalore, reaching Pakistan border, and back to Bangalore. In my beloved Seltos.

This post is very very detailed with like ~400 photos. Take your time, savour it over several days.  
In part, this was written for myself so that I could reminisce my days of adventure.

<Stop title="Roaming around Pune" waypoint="pune">

Spent the next day roaming around Pune with a friend (my junior from prev workplace), who hosted me at his place for some authentic breakfast, and showed me around. There was upma, "Nachni" - made from Ragi, and chilli oil. Did I mention they REALLY like spicy food? We then headed to a local poha place - which you guessed it, comes with spicy water (very tasty tho, no complaints) and roamed around a bit.

Pune feels very Marathi. I'd say it's like the Mysore of Maharashtra, the cultural capital if you will. Shivaji Maharaj is well revered and the food and culture is unique. Doesn't feel like a generic Indian city.

An interesting fact I learned during this trip was that there were very many good hotels in the middle of nowhere along the highway. I often thought I had to make pit stops at larger cities, but that wasn't necessary. The roads started to get really crappy again, and oh god the amount of trucks will kill you. Once you enter Gujarat, the roads get super good again, but it's allll truck traffic. Yay for economy but so very boring.

<Stop title="The land of Cheese" waypoint="ahmedabad">

Amboli -> Ahmedabad

On the way is the NE1 - National Expressway 1. I SWEAR I've not found a single speed limit board on this expressway. It was an elevated access-controlled 4 lane with boards only saying be careful, and not "do not go beyond 80 or 120" or whatever. I did indeed hit healthy triple digit speeds here and no challan came my way. India's own Autobahn!
90+ km of Baroda to Ahmedabad happened in no time.

I spent a decent amount of time in Ahmedabad. The infra was good, city seemed peaceful, I could see a lot of "development". The people seemed chill and non-confrontational, no "bullet machoism", and overall pretty lean. A very "neutral" looking Indian if I could say so.

Also, it's Khaman, not Dhokla. And there are very many varieties of it! I visited this Khaman shop where the shopkeeper was friendly and gave me a sampler plate (which isn't part of the menu, and often denied). I LOVE it!

There's a very interesting temple on the way to Jodhpur with a super interesting deity - yes, a motorcycle!

The story is that the rider Om Singh Rathore happened to have crashed and died at the location of this temple, following which the police impounded this bike and took it to the police station. However, the next morning the bike magically had re-appeared at the crash site and the police had to drain the fuel and take it back again along with locking it up. And yet again the bike had mysteriously re-appeared at the same location, following a cycle of locking it up and it re-appearing again - the locals had then believed that this bike had supernatural powers.
I was told that this information was logged in the police records.

<TripPhoto src="IMG_7526.jpg" caption="Bullet Baba" />

Travelers stop here to seek blessings and pray for a successful journey. I found the whole experience very fascinating! Hinduism has 33 crore gods and 1 Royal Enfield Bullet 350cc.

<Stop title="Longewala: history" waypoint="longewala">

If you aren't aware, Longewala is the place where a very important engagement happened between India and Pakistan during the 1971 Bangladesh liberation war. 120 Indian soldiers apparently held this border post against 2000-3000 Pak soldiers + tanks (read more: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Longewala).

There's a good amount of captured artifacts from the battle. It's always cool to get glimpses of the army. A LOT of strategy is often involved, and it's not just brute strength.
