This one's going to be a looooooooong one. \<FIXME cringe> This trip is the crown jewel of my _soloyolos_<Fn n={1} />, and this post is the crown jewel of this website. There's a ton of tech behind the scenes!

I had been fantasising about this trip for a good few years - a Gujarat Rajasthan trip, in my own vehicle, free of any worry in the world. Everything aligned in Dec 2023 and a happier chubbier me got to thoroughly experience this.

Lasted 17 days, 5500 km, starting from Bangalore, reaching Pakistan border, and back to Bangalore. In my beloved Seltos. What an incredible mile muncher.

This post is very detailed, with like ~400 photos. Take your time, savour it over several days. In part, this was written for future me so that I could reminisce my days of adventure.

<Stop title="Roaming around Pune" waypoint="pune">

Spent the next day roaming around Pune with a friend (my junior from prev workplace), who hosted me at his place for some authentic breakfast, and showed me around. There was upma, "Nachni" - made from Ragi, and chilli oil. Did I mention they REALLY like spicy food? We then headed to a local poha place - which you guessed it, comes with spicy water (very tasty tho, no complaints) and roamed around a bit.

Pune feels very Marathi. I'd say it's like the Mysore of Maharashtra, the cultural capital if you will. Shivaji Maharaj is well revered and the food and culture is unique. Doesn't feel like a generic Indian city.

<Stop title="Towards Gujarat" waypoint="amboli">

Pune -> skip Mumbai -> Amboli

An interesting fact I learned during this trip was that there were very many good hotels in the middle of nowhere along the highway. I often thought I had to make pit stops at larger cities, but that wasn't necessary. There was this really cool hotel somewhere between Mumbai and Gujarat border that offered a nice clean small room, and good food. Because of this, I could skip all of Mumbai (which I had been to a couple years ago). Also the Pune -> Mumbai highway is super good (with very many cameras) and a nice food stop.
The roads started to get really crappy again, and oh god the amount of trucks will kill you. Once you enter Gujarat, the roads get super good again, but it's allll truck traffic. Very boring.

There's a very interesting temple on the way to Jodhpur with a super interesting deity - yes, a motorcycle!

The story is that the rider Om Singh Rathore happened to have crashed and died at the location of this temple, following which the police impounded this bike and took it to the police station. However, the next morning the bike magically had re-appeared at the crash site and the police had to drain the fuel and take it back again along with locking it up. And yet again the bike had mysteriously re-appeared at the same location, following a cycle of locking it up and it re-appearing it again - the locals had then believed that this bike had supernatural powers.
I was told that this information was logged in the police records.

<TripPhoto src="IMG_7526.jpg" caption="Bullet Baba" />

Travelers stop here to seek blessings and pray for a successful journey. I found the whole experience very fascinating! Hinduism has 33 crore gods and 1 Royal Enfield Bullet 350cc.

<Stop title="Bishnoi Village" waypoint="bishnoi">
Om Banna Temple -> Bishnoi Village near Jodhpur

On the way to Jodhpur I came across an interesting village of the Bishnoi community. Some interesting history to the place - read up on the "Khejarli Massacre" - where very many villagers who were hugging trees to save them from being axed were killed by the Maharaja.

Got to try some what-appeared-to-be authentic Rajasthani food - Daal bati churma, Bhajra Rotlo (iirc name), Ker Sangri - desert beans dish, Gatte ki sabzi, Kadhi, and Kalmi vada. Pretty good!

I wasn't satisfied still. I wanted desert desert. I want to see them sand dunes. Towards "Sam" I go.

<Stop title="The Sam incident" waypoint="sam">

Sooo I ddi not really have a tent booked for this place. Walked in to a few and asked if there were any rooms / tents available. Most said no - except one guy. He claimed there was in fact a tent, set up on the dunes, with "all amenities", and that I could get it for a sizeable amount.

Said yes without thinking twice.
The stay came with a culture show, food, water etc.

At around 11, I hopped on their jeep heading towards this "tent" and lo behold - there's been miscommunication. There's no tent. There's no "amenities". There's NOTHING.

Then one of the local helper dudes told me he'd sleep on the floor (???) and let me have his charpoy.
So my stay ended up being a charpoy and 2 thick blankets.

One the most INSANE experiences of my life. I had no bag, clothes, nothing. Was at least wearing my hoodie and that's about it. Mind you - it gets insanely cold at night in the desert. The temperatures are extreme.

This place was a good few kms from any kind civilisation. Was awoken at 2 AM ish to try and photograph a low light pollution sky - but I could barely move. Mind numbing winds - quite literally. Far too cold to lift a finger even.

Come morning I found a group of locals who were warming themselves with a fire, and waiting for the sunrise - and my jeep to arrive. Don't think I would try it again, but god what a fun experience.
